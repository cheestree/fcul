    \begin{abstract}
        Biological Control Agents (BCAs) provide an increasingly important alternative to conventional chemical
        plant protection products.

        However, information regarding their applicability to specific crop-pathogen
        combinations remains fragmented across diverse regulatory and agricultural data sources.

        This fragmentation, together with inconsistencies in terminology and data representation, makes the
        identification of suitable BCAs difficult and limits the development of automated decision-support systems.

        This work presents an approach for identifying biological control agents for crop-pathogen combinations
        through data integration, rule based validation, and authorization informed ranking.
        Data from Portugal's SIFITO and the European Union pesticide database are parsed, normalized, and integrated
        into a common representation connecting crops, target organisms, authorized products, and microbial active
        substances.

        The current prototype uses the integrated authorization data to distinguish BCA-related rows from non-BCA
        rows and to rank candidate microbe-product records for a queried crop-pathogen pair.
        The evaluation therefore measures recovery of authorization-derived labels and known regulatory
        associations, rather than independent biological efficacy.

    \end{abstract}

    \section{Background and Context}\label{sec:background-and-context}

    Agricultural production is continuously affected by pests and plant diseases which contribute substantially to
    crop losses worldwide.

    Crop protection has therefore become an essential component of modern agricultural
    production, with chemical pesticides remaining one of the primary mechanisms for controlling harmful organisms.

    Despite their effectiveness, extensive reliance on chemical plant protection products raises concerns regarding
    soil quality, pathogen resistance, crop quality and animal population.

    These concerns, together with increasing demand for sustainable agricultural practices, have motivated the
    development and adoption of alternative crop-protection strategies.


    \section{Motivation and Project Framing}\label{sec:motivation-and-project-framing}

    Although information regarding BCAs and their authorized uses is available through existing regulatory
    resources, identifying suitable biological control options for a particular crop-pathogen combination remains a
    non-trivial task.
    Relevant information is distributed across many sources that differ in structure, terminology
    and scope.

    Furthermore, these systems are primarily designed to expose regulatory or production information
    rather than to support the discovery of biological control alternatives for a given agricultural problem.
    This motivates treating BCA identification as a recommendation problem.

    Rather than requiring users to manually
    navigate individual products and regulatory records, known relationships between crops, pathogens, products and
    active substances can be represented and exploited to rank candidate biological control options for a given
    crop-pathogen pair.


    \section{Research Goals}\label{sec:research-goals}

    The primary goal of this work is to investigate whether heterogeneous agricultural and regulatory data can be integrated and exploited to support the recommendation of Biological Control Agents for crop-pathogen combinations.
    To address this objective, the project is structured around the following goals.

    First, the work aims to unify data collected from heterogeneous sources into a common and consistent representation.
    These sources may differ in structure, terminology, naming conventions, and level of detail, requiring the normalization of crops, pathogens, products, active substances, and their corresponding relationships.
    The resulting representation should provide a suitable basis for both analysis and recommendation.

    Second, a query and ranking prototype is developed to identify candidate BCAs for a given crop-pathogen
    combination.
    The prototype exploits known authorization records and an authorization triage score, allowing relevant candidates
    to be ranked according to the information available in the integrated dataset.

    Third, the effectiveness of the current approach is evaluated using pipeline outputs and held-out authorization
    rows.
    This evaluation aims to determine whether the model can reproduce the distinction between BCA-related and non-BCA
    authorization records, while making clear that this is not a biological efficacy validation.

    A further goal is to support the continuous incorporation of newly available data.
    New records should pass through the same filtering, normalization, and validation pipeline before being incorporated into the recommendation dataset.
    Following these updates, the model can be retrained and reevaluated to determine whether triage and ranking
    behavior remain consistent as the underlying data evolves.

    Finally, the resulting prototype is intended to be integrated into the PlantPathoRec platform, allowing the
    developed approach to be exposed as part of a broader decision-support environment for plant pathology and crop
    protection.

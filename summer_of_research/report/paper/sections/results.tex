\section{Results}\label{sec:results-and-project-outcomes}

\subsection{Pipeline outputs and data composition}

After parsing, normalisation, reference matching, and completeness filtering, the joined modelling data contain 195,197 complete rows: 11,690 rows in the BCA-related \textit{final} or \textit{review} states and 183,507 \textit{discarded} rows with no detected BCA signal.
The ranking catalogue uses 1,530 accepted Portuguese \textit{final} rows.
The ranking catalogue is limited to Portuguese rows because this source provides the complete scientific crop--pathogen fields and linked evidence required by the evaluation. The EU, Spanish, and French exports remain part of the integrated resource, but they do not provide the same safe product--use or scientific pair links; including them in the ranking catalogue would require inferred relations.
After standardising microbial values, these rows contain 24 BCA values, 27 scientific pathogens, 166 scientific crops, and 36 products.
They form 361 complete crop--pathogen contexts and 595 unique context--BCA interactions.
A row is a source record; an interaction is the aggregated presence of one standardised BCA in one scientific crop--pathogen context, so the two counts should not be read as the same unit.

\subsection{Screening-model results}

The four screening-model metrics are $1.000$ on the grouped held-out set. These high values are expected from an outcome-reproduction
task: the BCA-related and no-signal outcomes are generated by the same pipeline rules applied to the records, while fields such
as the microbial active substance, product name, and product function provide strong clues to those outcomes. The grouped
split keeps repeated dataset--microbe--pathogen--product keys together, but it does not remove related source vocabulary
or make the screening outcomes independent of the input records.

Table~\ref{tab:screening-model-metrics} reports the screening-model metrics on the held-out set.

\subsection{Ranking results}

Table~\ref{tab:ranking-results} reports results for inferred candidates. The variants are score comparisons over the same
held-out cases, not different data splits. \textit{Hybrid} uses the full fixed weighted score, combining exact-context,
context, pathogen, and collaborative support. \textit{Direct support} removes the collaborative component and uses
only the direct authorisation signals. \textit{Collaborative} uses only the item-similarity score. \textit{Pathogen
    baseline} ranks candidates by pathogen-level support, while \textit{Total baseline} ranks them by support across all
contexts. After each ranking, the view of inferred candidates removes candidates with an exact query-context match; this is why
the table evaluates candidates that are supported by related evidence rather than already known for the queried pair.
The table separates candidate recovery, measured by
coverage and HitRate@k, from rank quality, measured by MRR and NDCG@10. Coverage is the fraction of queries in
which the held-out BCA remains in the candidate catalogue. HitRate@k is the fraction of queries that recover it in
the first $k$ positions. MRR rewards higher ranks, and NDCG@10 gives less weight to lower-ranked hits.

In the all-candidate view, the hybrid ranker reaches 100.0\% coverage, 81.7\% Hit@5, and an MRR of 0.319. The
view of inferred candidates is the user-facing analysis shown in Table~\ref{tab:ranking-results}.

The screening score is not included in the current ranking score. This keeps it available as transparent
evidence from the authorisation data while avoiding a ranking contribution that was not supported by the held out
retrieval results. The results should therefore be read as recovery of associations present in the source data,
not as evidence that one BCA is biologically superior to another.

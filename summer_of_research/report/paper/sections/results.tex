\section{Results}\label{sec:results-and-project-outcomes}

The four classifier metrics are $1.000$ on the grouped holdout. This reflects the strong separation between the
pipeline-generated classes in the input record fields, as explained in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-design}. It shows
reproduction of the pipeline labels in this split, not independent biological validation or efficacy.

Table~\ref{tab:ranking-results} reports the inferred-only results. The variants are scoring ablations over the same
held-out cases, not different data splits. \textit{Hybrid} uses the full fixed weighted score, combining exact-context,
context, pathogen, and collaborative support. \textit{Direct support} removes the collaborative component and uses
only the direct authorisation signals. \textit{Collaborative} uses only the item-similarity score. \textit{Pathogen
    baseline} ranks candidates by pathogen-level support, while \textit{Total baseline} ranks them by support across all
contexts. After each ranking, the inferred-only view removes candidates with an exact query-context match; this is why
the table evaluates candidates that are supported by related evidence rather than already known for the queried pair.
The table separates candidate recovery, measured by
coverage and HitRate@k, from rank quality, measured by MRR and NDCG@10. Coverage is the fraction of queries in
which the held-out BCA remains in the candidate catalogue. HitRate@k is the fraction of queries that recover it in
the first $k$ positions. MRR rewards higher ranks, and NDCG@10 gives less weight to lower-ranked hits.

In the all-candidate view, the hybrid ranker reaches 100.0\% coverage, 81.7\% Hit@5, and an MRR of 0.319. The
inferred-only view is the user-facing analysis shown in Table~\ref{tab:ranking-results}.

The triage score is not included in the current ranking score. This keeps it available as transparent
evidence from the authorisation data while avoiding a ranking contribution that was not supported by the held out
retrieval results. The results should therefore be read as recovery of associations present in the source data,
not as evidence that one BCA is biologically superior to another.
